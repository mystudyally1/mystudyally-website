Most tutor searches start with the subject: "we need a maths tutor." That is
the right instinct and an incomplete brief. A tutor can be excellent at maths
and still be the wrong fit for your child's exam.

## Subject knowledge is the floor, not the ceiling

Someone with a maths degree knows more maths than any A Level paper will ask
for. That does not mean they know how the paper is marked, which topics carry
the most marks, or where the examiner expects working to be shown.

Exam performance rewards a specific skill: producing what the mark scheme
credits, under time pressure. That skill is learned from the specification and
the past papers, not from the subject in the abstract.

## The questions worth asking

**Which exam board have you taught this subject for?**
The useful answer names a board and a paper, not "all of them." Cambridge,
Edexcel, AQA, and OCR differ in question style and topic weighting.

**Have you taught this at this level, recently?**
GCSE Physics and A Level Physics are different jobs. So are the 2018
specification and the one before it. Recency matters because specifications
change.

**How do you use past papers?**
A good answer involves diagnosis — using papers to find the specific gaps —
rather than working through them front to back for practice.

**What do you do when a student is stuck?**
You are listening for a method, not reassurance. Re-explaining the same
explanation more slowly is not a method.

## Signals that matter more than credentials

- **They ask about the school first.** A tutor who wants to know what the
  school is already covering is planning to complement it, not duplicate it.
- **They talk about the mark scheme unprompted.** This is a strong signal of
  exam-focused teaching.
- **They are specific about what they don't cover.** A tutor who says "Further
  Maths isn't my area" is more trustworthy than one who claims everything.

## On trial sessions

Use them properly. A trial is not a formality — it is the only direct evidence
you will get before committing. Watch for whether your child talks during the
session or only listens, and whether they can explain something afterwards that
they could not explain before.

If a provider will not offer a trial before payment, that tells you something
too.

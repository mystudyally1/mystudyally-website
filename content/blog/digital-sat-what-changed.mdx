The SAT moved to a digital, adaptive format internationally in 2023 and in the
United States in 2024. If your preparation materials predate that, they are
teaching a test that no longer exists.

## What actually changed

**It is shorter.** Around two hours instead of three, across two sections:
Reading and Writing, then Math.

**It is section-adaptive.** Each section comes in two modules. Performance on
the first module determines the difficulty of the second. This is adaptive at
the *module* level, not question by question — so within a module you can still
skip and come back.

**Reading passages are shorter.** Instead of long passages with many questions
each, there are many short passages with a single question each. This is the
change students notice most.

**Calculators are allowed throughout Math**, and Desmos is built into the
testing app.

**Scoring is still out of 1600**, so scores remain comparable to the old test.

## What this means for preparation

### Pacing has to be relearned

The old strategy of triaging a long passage — skim, then hunt for answers —
does not transfer. With one question per short passage, the efficient approach
is to read the passage properly once. Students trained on the old format
frequently over-skim and lose marks they would otherwise get.

### The first module carries extra weight

Because performance in module one determines which module two you receive, a
weak start constrains the score you can reach. That makes early-question
accuracy and settling quickly more important than they were on the paper test.

### Use the real testing app

The College Board's Bluebook application is what students sit the test in.
Practising in it — rather than on paper printouts — matters, because the
annotation tools, the built-in Desmos calculator, and the on-screen reading
experience all take adjustment.

### Old practice tests have limited value

Pre-2023 paper tests still contain useful maths and grammar practice, but they
misrepresent the reading section's structure and the overall pacing. Full-length
paper mocks are the least transferable part.

## A reasonable preparation shape

1. Sit a full adaptive practice test in Bluebook to get a genuine baseline.
2. Diagnose by question type rather than by score alone.
3. Drill the weak types in short, frequent sessions.
4. Re-test full-length periodically — adaptive, in the app, timed.

## If you are getting help

Ask directly whether the tutor prepares students for the digital adaptive
format specifically. It is a fair question, and the answer should be
unambiguous. Preparation built around the old paper SAT will feel productive
and leave your child rehearsing for a test they will not sit.

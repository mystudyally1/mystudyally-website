An IELTS result arrives as five numbers: one for each of Listening, Reading,
Writing and Speaking, plus an overall band. The overall is the one universities
quote, but it is the four underneath that tell you what to do next.

## How the overall band is built

The overall band is the average of the four skill scores, rounded to the nearest
half band. That rounding is where most of the confusion lives.

Averages ending in .25 round **up** to the next half band; averages ending in .75
round up to the next whole band. So a student sitting on 6.375 gets a 6.5, and a
student on 6.125 gets a 6.0 — a difference of a quarter of a mark in one skill.

The practical consequence: when a student is close to a threshold, a single half
band in one skill can move the overall. That makes the weakest skill the highest
leverage place to work, almost always.

## What the common thresholds mean

Most UK undergraduate courses ask for **6.0 or 6.5 overall**. Competitive or
essay-heavy courses often ask for 7.0. Many also set a **minimum in each
component**, which is the requirement students most often miss.

This is worth reading carefully. "6.5 overall with no band below 6.0" means a
student with 7.5 Listening, 7.0 Reading, 7.0 Speaking and 5.5 Writing does *not*
meet the offer, despite a 6.5 overall. The average is fine; the Writing minimum
is not.

Check the exact wording of the offer before planning any retake.

## Which skill to work on

**Writing is usually the lowest**, and usually the most improvable, because most
of the marks lost are structural rather than linguistic. Task 1 wants an accurate
summary of data with no invented explanation; Task 2 wants a clear position held
consistently across the essay. Students who address only part of the prompt cap
their Task Response mark regardless of vocabulary.

**Speaking** rewards fluency and coherence more than complexity. Long silences
and self-corrections cost more than the occasional wrong word.

**Reading and Listening** are the most trainable by technique — question-type
strategy and timing rather than English ability. Students who lose marks here
under time pressure often gain a half band from pacing work alone.

## Retake or not

Two questions decide it:

1. **Is the gap in one skill or spread across all four?** A single weak skill is a
   short, targeted piece of work. A flat profile a full band below target is a
   longer project.
2. **When is the offer deadline?** Results take a few days for computer-delivered
   tests and around two weeks for paper. Work backwards from the university's
   deadline before booking.

If the gap is half a band in one skill and there is time, a focused block on that
skill is usually the fastest route. If the gap is a full band across the board,
plan for a longer period of general English work — retaking the test quickly
tends to produce the same result.

Some schools enter students for GCSE in most subjects and IGCSE in one or two —
often the sciences or English. If you have been asked to express a preference,
the choice is narrower than it looks, and it rarely comes down to which
qualification is "better".

## They are equivalent where it counts

Both are taken around age 16, both are graded 9–1 on the boards most UK-linked
schools use, and both are accepted by sixth forms, colleges and universities. A
student with IGCSEs is not disadvantaged applying to a UK sixth form, and the
reverse is equally true.

Treat them as two routes to the same destination, then look at what actually
differs.

## What actually differs

**The exam board and specification.** GCSE runs through AQA, Edexcel, OCR, WJEC
and CCEA. IGCSE is mainly Cambridge International and Edexcel International. Two
students both "doing Physics" may sit genuinely different papers with different
topic coverage and different mark schemes.

**Assessment structure.** Reformed GCSEs in England are almost entirely
exam-assessed, with practical work examined through written questions. IGCSE
varies by board and subject — some retain a practical or coursework component,
some offer an alternative-to-practical paper.

**Tiering.** Many subjects in both systems are tiered. Cambridge IGCSE uses Core
and Extended; GCSE uses Foundation and Higher. Entry is capped at different
grades, and the decision is often made earlier than families expect.

## The questions worth asking the school

- Which board and specification will my child be entered for?
- Which tier, and when is that decision made?
- Is there a coursework or practical component, and how is it assessed?
- Does the department have more experience with one route than the other?

That last one matters more than the qualification. A department that has taught
the same IGCSE specification for a decade is usually a better bet than one
switching to GCSE this year, whatever the paperwork says.

## Where your child goes next

If they are staying in the same school through sixth form, ask which route feeds
more naturally into the A Level or IB courses on offer. If they are moving —
particularly internationally — check whether the receiving school has a
preference. Most do not. Some international schools are more familiar with
Cambridge, which occasionally makes admissions conversations simpler.

## For tutoring

Whichever route is chosen, the useful detail is the **board and tier**, not the
letter in front of GCSE. It is printed on the exam timetable and on any past
paper the school has issued. With those two facts, tutoring can use the right
past papers and the mark-scheme language the examiner actually rewards.

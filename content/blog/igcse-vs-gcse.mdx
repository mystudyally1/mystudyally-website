Parents ask us this more than almost anything else. The names are nearly
identical, the subjects overlap heavily, and schools rarely explain the
difference clearly. Here is what actually separates them.

## The short version

**GCSE** is the qualification taken by students in schools in England, Wales,
and Northern Ireland. **IGCSE** — the "I" is for International — is the version
designed for international schools, and it is taken in well over a hundred
countries.

Both are typically taken around age 16, both are graded 9–1 (or A*–G on some
IGCSE boards), and both are accepted by sixth forms and universities. A student
with strong IGCSEs is not at a disadvantage applying to a UK sixth form, and the
reverse is equally true.

## Where they genuinely differ

### Exam boards

GCSE is offered by AQA, Edexcel (Pearson), OCR, WJEC, and CCEA. IGCSE is
offered mainly by Cambridge International (CAIE) and Edexcel International.

This matters more than most people realise. Two students both "doing IGCSE
Physics" may be sitting genuinely different papers with different topic
coverage, different practical requirements, and different mark schemes. A tutor
who knows Cambridge 0625 inside out is not automatically fluent in Edexcel
International Physics.

### Coursework and practicals

Reformed GCSEs in England are almost entirely exam-assessed, with practical
work assessed through written questions rather than marked coursework. IGCSE
varies by board and subject — some retain a coursework or practical assessment
component, some offer an alternative-to-practical written paper.

If your child's grade depends partly on coursework, that changes how tutoring
time is best spent.

### Tiering

Many subjects in both systems are tiered: a Foundation paper capped at a lower
grade, and a Higher paper that reaches the top grades. Cambridge IGCSE uses
Core and Extended.

Tier entry is a decision with real consequences, and it is often made earlier
than families expect. If your child is borderline, that is worth raising with
the school — and worth telling a tutor, because preparing for Extended is a
different job from securing a solid Core grade.

## What this means practically

The question that matters is not "IGCSE or GCSE?" — that is usually decided by
the school your child attends. The question is **which board, and which tier**.

When you know those two things, tutoring becomes far more targeted: past papers
from the right series, mark-scheme language that matches what the examiner
actually rewards, and topic coverage that matches the specification rather than
a generic syllabus.

If you are not sure which board your child sits, it is printed on their exam
timetable and on any past paper the school has issued. Ask the subject teacher
if in doubt — and tell us when you inquire, so we match accordingly.

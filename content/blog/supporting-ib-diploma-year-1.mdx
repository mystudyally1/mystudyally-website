The first year of the IB Diploma is a step up in breadth rather than difficulty.
Students who coped comfortably with eight or nine IGCSEs suddenly find six
subjects plus Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and CAS competing for the
same evenings. The problem is almost never capability. It is load management.

## What the first year actually demands

**Six subjects, three at Higher Level.** HL subjects carry noticeably more
content and go deeper. Most of the strain in Year 1 comes from HL choices made in
the first few weeks, sometimes before a student knows what the subject involves.

**Internal Assessments start early.** Each subject carries an IA — a lab
investigation, a maths exploration, a commentary — worth a meaningful share of
the final grade. Deadlines cluster, and they are set by the school, not the IB.

**TOK and the Extended Essay run in the background.** Neither has weekly homework
in the usual sense, which is exactly why they slip. The EE in particular is a
4,000-word independent piece that rewards early, steady work and punishes a late
start.

## Where parents can actually help

**Ask about the calendar, not the grades.** The single most useful thing in Year
1 is a shared view of when IA drafts and the EE milestones are due. Most schools
publish this. Put it somewhere visible.

**Watch the HL/SL fit in the first term.** If a student is drowning in one HL
subject by October half term, a change is usually still possible and far less
costly than persisting for a year. Ask the coordinator early — the window closes.

**Protect one evening.** Diploma students who work every night without a break
tend to lose efficiency by spring. A protected evening is not lost study time; it
is what makes the other six sustainable.

**Treat CAS as scheduling, not achievement.** CAS is a requirement to complete,
not a competition. Students who treat it as another thing to excel at add load
for no marks.

## Where tutoring fits

The common Year 1 pattern is one subject dragging — often Maths AA HL, a science
HL, or English A for students newer to literary analysis. Targeted support in
that one subject frees attention for the other five, and is usually more
effective than general study-skills help.

The IA is the other place support pays. Not writing it — that would be
malpractice, and schools check — but helping a student narrow a research question
to something answerable in the word count. A vague question is the most common
reason an IA underperforms.

## What not to worry about yet

Predicted grades, university lists, and final points totals belong to Year 2.
Year 1 is about establishing a workload rhythm that survives the second year,
when the EE, IAs and revision genuinely overlap. A student who ends Year 1 tired
but organised is in better shape than one who ended it with slightly higher marks
and no system.

Most families ask about tutoring in one of two windows: just after mock results
land in January, or in a panic around Easter. The first is usually right. The
second is usually late — not too late to help, but late enough that the work
changes from building understanding to triage.

Here is how the year actually runs, and where support does the most good.

## Work backwards from the exam calendar

Main written papers for GCSE, IGCSE and A Level sit across May and June.
Everything before that is preparation with a deadline attached:

- **September–November.** New content is being taught. Tutoring here is about
  keeping pace, not catching up. Cheapest time to fix a shaky foundation.
- **November–December.** Mock preparation. The first honest signal of where a
  student actually is.
- **January.** Mock results. The single best moment to act — there is still a
  full term of teaching left, and you now have evidence rather than a hunch.
- **February–March.** The productive middle. Content gaps can still be closed
  properly and exam technique built on top.
- **April–May.** Revision and past papers. Real gains are possible, but they come
  from technique and recall, not from learning topics for the first time.

## The mistake at each end

**Starting too late** compresses two jobs into one. A student who does not yet
understand moments in physics cannot be taught exam technique for moments — the
technique has nothing to sit on. By late April there is rarely time to do both
well, so something gets skipped.

**Starting too early**, with no clear goal, tends to fade. Weekly sessions
beginning in September with no diagnosis and no target often become homework
supervision. That has value, but it is not what most families are paying for, and
motivation drops by February.

## A reasonable rule

If mocks revealed a specific, nameable problem — a topic, a paper, a question
type — start within a few weeks of the results. Momentum from a concrete goal
carries well through spring.

If the issue is broader, such as a subject the student has been behind in for a
year, start earlier and expect the first month to be diagnosis rather than
progress.

And if you are reading this in April: still worth doing. Just be clear with the
tutor that the goal is marks on the paper, not complete coverage, and let them
tell you where the remaining time is best spent.
